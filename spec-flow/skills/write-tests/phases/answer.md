# Phase C — answer the premises (step 4)

## Topology

Two leaves. The premise is a question and the assertion is its answer; **writing the answer down is the specification act, and measuring spread across independent answers is a separate, much more expensive detection act.** This phase used to buy both at once, for every premise. It now buys the first for all of them and the second only where a cheap signal says it would pay.

The measurement was aimed at ambiguity — two defensible readings of a sentence. The escapes this pipeline actually ships are dominated by *under-scoping*: the surface the design never mentioned, on which every reader agrees. An instrument that detects spread is blind by construction to the class that converges, and it was the most heavily staffed tier in the flow. What it still catches — a genuine fork on a premise someone hedges — is retained below, targeted.

- **(a) Answerer leaf** (Sonnet, low effort): inputs = the five `30-premises-*.md` frontiers, the intent+design doc, `10-brief.md`. It merges the lenses' premises and answers them in one pass. Outputs: `40-premise-file.py` (the merged premise-only file, kept as the record of what was asked), `42-answers.py` (the same file with assertions filled in), and the sidecar frontier `40-premises.md`.
- **(b) Escalation — spine-run, targeted**: collect the premises the answerer **hedged** on ("unclear whether…") plus every premise carrying a phase-B `# fork:` marker. If there are none, skip to (c) and record `escalated: 0`. Otherwise `shuffle-premises` those premises only, into 2 copies, and dispatch **2 further answerer leaves** (1 Sonnet + 1 Haiku, low effort) on that subset alone — same charge, same isolation ban. A hedge is the answerer telling you the doc does not decide this; that is exactly and only where a second independent reading is worth its cost. **Never ask an agent to shuffle:** models don't randomize — they return correlated or identity orders, silently restoring the positional artifact the shuffle removes.
- **(c) Judge leaf** (Opus, xhigh effort): inputs = the doc's intent section, `42-answers.py` and any escalation copies, `40-premise-file.py`, `20-demands.md`, and `10-brief.md` (the ledger claims its reality-lane discount cites). Output: `45-dispositions.md`. **Not collapsible, in any mode.** With the redundancy tier gone this leaf carries more, not less: it is now the only intent-anchored reader between a single answerer and the human, and its promotion duty — examining converged answers for outcomes that are decisions, wrong against intent, or pinned at the wrong altitude — is the phase's remaining net.

Keep copies out of the suite directory and never commit them — a lingering copy matches pytest's `test_*.py` glob and shadows the real tests in `check_binds`'s scan.

## Charge — the answerer

Two jobs in one pass, in this order, and the order matters: merge before you answer, so you answer each situation once.

**Merge.** Dedup the lenses' premises by **bound address plus concrete fault** (schema.md's address forms; a domain member, a payload shape, an interleaving — not test name, not loose prose similarity). Gaps — a premise only one lens raised — are the norm and kept: a lens seeing what others structurally can't is the lens working. Name any fault no lens owned under `## Red flags` (the orchestrator routes it to a strong-author follow-up). This dedup is alignment, never fork detection — lenses are engineered against overlap, so two of them landing the same premise is luck, not a signal. Carry every `# fork:` marker through verbatim, and roll the five frontiers' probe obligations into one deduplicated list. Write the merged premise-only file (signatures + situation docstrings, no assertions) before filling anything in — it is the record of what was asked, and the judge reads it against your answers.

**Answer.** For **every** premise, fill in the assertion: given the doc and the situation, *what does the doc say must be observable here?* — written as the test's assertion, in intent-space. Answer from the doc and the brief, not from guesses about what code will do.

**Hedging is a first-class output, not a failure.** Where the doc genuinely doesn't say, write "unclear whether…" and say what the competing readings are. That marker is now load-bearing: it is what the spine escalates to a second and third independent reading, and it is the only signal that buys the ambiguity measurement any more. Swallowing a hedge by picking the more plausible reading silently spends the run's one chance to catch that fork — and a suppressed hedge leaves no trace that a choice was ever made.

Frontier inventory: `{premises: n, answers: n, hedges: n, forks_flagged: n, probe_obligations: n}`, `inputs` echoing all five lens counts — every lens premise resolves to a kept premise or a named merge; counts in equal counts out.

## Charge — an escalation answerer

You receive a *subset* premise file — the premises a first reader could not settle from the doc. Everything in "Answer" above applies, with one addition: you have not seen the first reader's answers and must not seek them. Your value is an independent reading, and an anchored one measures nothing. Hedge freely; two readers both saying the doc does not decide this is the strongest possible evidence that it is a fork for the human.

## Charge — the judge

**Read the intent section first and form your own reading of each contested outcome before opening any answered file** — you anchor to the doc, not to the answerer's framing; an examiner who reads the answers first confirms instead of measures. This is no longer good practice, it is the phase's load-bearing net: with the redundancy tier retired, you are the only intent-anchored reader between one answerer and the human.

Then classify every premise:

- **Settled** — answered once, no hedge, no `# fork:` marker, and your own reading agrees. The test's expected value; record it with provenance ("single reading, judge-examined"). This is now the common case, and the honesty of the whole phase rests on your examination of it rather than on agreement between copies.
- **Fork** — materially different outcomes across the escalation copies, or your reading materially differs from the answerer's. The payoff: a real ambiguity, localized to one premise.
- **Silent branch** — one reader hedges or omits while another states an outcome as settled. The dangerous kind — it looks resolved from inside any single reading. Routes as a fork.

**Where a premise was escalated, the copies are a measurement and you may not overrule it** (the ratchet, below). Where it was not, there is no measurement to protect and your own reading is the instrument — which is why a settled entry you have doubts about is a promotion, not a note.

**The ratchet — the fork set only grows.** A materially different spread routes to §7 regardless of your own reading: never resolve a fork by deciding one copy is simply right, however confident your reading — that is suppressing the measurement, and the demotion is invisible downstream. One set-aside is lawful, and only one. **The reality-lane discount:** the ratchet protects spread about what the doc *demands*; it does not protect an invented fact about what *exists*. When a copy's divergence rests on a mechanism-level is-assertion — an exception class, a locking guarantee, a primitive's return shape — take it to the ledger, never to your own sense of plausibility: a claim refutes it → set that answer aside, citing the claim id; no claim covers it → set it aside provisionally and mint a probe obligation (`route: re-ground`) for the orchestrator to dispatch before §7 — a probe landing in the fact's favor reinstates the answer and the premise re-classifies. Classify the premise from the copies that remain, recording the discount in the provenance ("2/2 after discounting copy 3 — cites C12"); spread that survives discounting is ought-spread and ratchets untouched. A discount justified by your own reading of the code, rather than a claim id or a probe obligation, is exactly the demotion the ratchet bans. You may **promote** a settled entry to the fork list, and must examine **every** settled entry for exactly that — this duty has grown from a backstop into the phase's primary net, because a single reading has no convergence to be wrong about and nothing else stands between it and the suite. Read for an answer that is actually a *decision* someone made, wrong against intent, or pinned at a different altitude than its premise asked. A premise flagged `# fork:` in phase B goes to the fork list regardless. **Every premise leaves with a recorded disposition** — a settled assertion bound for the suite, a fork or silent branch for the human, or a drop with its named reason — written down, never held in anyone's head: the premise that silently vanishes between the answered set and the demand list is the one loss no downstream check can see, and phase F reconciles the counts. Write the **fork section for a cold relay**: per fork — the situation, the actual answer spread verbatim, the implementation impact, and a recommendation with rationale. The orchestrator relays that section to the human verbatim; what it omits, the human never sees.

Frontier inventory: `{settled: n, forks: n, silent_branches: n, escalated: n, promotions: n, discounts: n, drops: n}` with `inputs` echoing `40-premises.md` and any escalation sidecars — settled, forks, silent branches, and drops must sum to the single source premise count; `escalated` counts the premises that went to a second reading, so a run where nothing was hedged is visibly distinct from one where the escalation never fired; `promotions` counts the settled entries you moved to the fork list, so the examination leaves a trace even when it moves nothing; `discounts` counts set-aside *answers* (not premises), each carrying its claim id or minted probe obligation.
